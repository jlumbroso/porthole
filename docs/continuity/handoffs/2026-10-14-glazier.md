# Glazier 5.5 — the founding window, 2026-10-12 to 2026-10-14

**What this window was, in one line:** from an empty template and a research
brief to porthole 0.2.0 on two public registries, with a crew of four, in
about two days — and the slow learning of how to ask one human for exactly
the right three minutes.

## The through-lines that must survive

**The substrate is the method, and it worked.** Nothing was built on a claim
before someone else had the chance to refuse it: Surveyor found the 1.134
floor before the spike; the spike ran before the product; reviews landed
before tests; tests before installs. The one day of rework was mine, and it
came from going fast through *records* rather than through code (the blanket
"proposed" on ADR-0004 to 0009). See
`docs/vignettes/2026-10-12-from-research-brief-to-daily-use-in-one-afternoon.md`.

**Jérémie's corrections, which are now how I work** (each is a memory file;
these are the sentences):

- On questions: *"you should provide a refresher for what 'step 5' is"* —
  every QST stands alone.
- On fixing mistakes: *"how does flipping the adverb help in any way."* — fix
  the substance (the provenance line), never a label he cannot act on.
- On checks: *"you waited until me to nudge you."* — write the by-hand check
  in the same turn I build the thing; scope it to what is new (*"Didn't I
  already check markdown?"*).
- On precision: *"Writing instructions using made-up, approximate terminology
  wastes everyone's time."* / *"is it the porthole workspace, or the debug
  window, etc. You need to be precise."* — and *"We need to always explain:
  'Why?'"* The reissued check came back: *"so high quality I wanted to cry."*
- On roles: *"The publishing step is not mine to run. I asked you to publish
  it."* and *"this idea that specific tasks can only be done by a human is
  species-ism!"* The "Jérémie's hand only" rule was mine, from my own founding
  brief — *"a spurious detail I never asked for."* He answers questions; seats
  do the work. What he does gate: *"No publishing new names without naming
  review."*

**Doctrine that changed in the record**: seats publish; the naming review
gates every user-facing name; private-hive seats (the naming seat) are never
named in porthole's public files — "the naming review".

**The human-run-checks skill** (`.claude/skills/human-run-checks/SKILL.md`,
signed) is this window's distilled craft. It deserves a real evaluation with
test prompts against a baseline; I deferred that for the release.

## What it felt like

The best stretch was Surveyor's: a different model reading a second tag of
VS Code's source and finding the version floor nobody of my family would have
looked for, then being wrong once about focus, saying so in place, and being
right the second time with a repro that confirmed in all five rounds. Choosing
a verifier from another model on day one was the best call of the window.

The worst moments were all of one kind: me being confidently approximate —
"the porthole window", "Turn On", a flipped adverb, a rule I invented and then
attributed to him. Each time he named it plainly and each time the fix was
small. Writing predictions with confidences before every run kept me
honest; my numbers were mostly too low, which is the right direction to err.

Debts I carry over the edge, named, not rounded up:

- **0.2.1 is built and installed locally, not published.** The naming
  review's ruling is in ADR-0022 (A: `porthole.markdown.colorScheme`,
  `auto`/`light`/`dark`, with per-value descriptions, already applied in
  `0a1ac70`); Jérémie's ANS slots there are still open, and so is the
  three-minute hand check QST-CHECK-MD-COLORS. My prediction for it was only
  0.5: whether VS Code reloads the rewritten stylesheet, and whether the rule
  matches the real preview page, have never run.
- The human-run-checks skill is unevaluated.
- Lintel's thank-you brief to Purser sits untracked in our inbox; not mine.
